Project Overview

Nueva EPS needed to redesign its mobile app to improve how members accessed digital healthcare services. The previous version of the app had grown over time, but its navigation, login experience, and service structure had become difficult to understand.

The project focused on redesigning the app’s information architecture, access flow, and key self-service journeys, including medical service approvals, certificates, medical leave certificates and payment status, and healthcare network search.

My Role

My role was to lead the redesign of the mobile app experience, from research and information architecture to user flows and wireframes.

I worked on understanding how users expected the app’s content to be organised, redesigning the navigation structure, simplifying the login experience, and creating key screens for the main healthcare services.

My contribution included conducting a card sorting exercise, testing alternative login flows and defining the new app structure.

The Problem

The previous version of the app did not have a clear information architecture. New features had been added over time, but they were not organised around how users naturally searched for healthcare services.

This created a cluttered experience where users could struggle to understand where to go, especially when trying to complete important tasks such as checking appointments, downloading certificates, consulting authorisations, or finding healthcare providers.

Another key issue was the login flow. The previous version aimed to reduce the number of clicks, but the screen included too many options at once. This made the experience visually overloaded and confusing, especially for users who were not sure whether they already had an account or needed to register.

Research and Discovery

To understand how users expected the app content to be organised, I conducted an open card sorting exercise with 10 participants. The goal was to identify how users naturally grouped app features and which labels made the most sense to them.

Participants organised key app features into four predefined categories: Health, Certificates, Customer Support, and Settings. After the sessions, I analysed the results using a matrix that showed how many times each feature was placed under each category, as well as a percentage-based matrix to identify stronger and weaker classification patterns.

This gave the redesign a clearer structure and moved the app away from an overloaded hamburger menu. Instead, the new navigation used a bottom tab bar based on the categories identified through the card sorting exercise.

Information Architecture

Based on the card sorting results, I reorganised the app around clearer content areas that reflected how users expected to find services.

The new structure prioritised healthcare tasks and separated them from account settings and general customer service actions. This helped reduce ambiguity and made the app easier to scan.

Key Flows

Home and access logic

I treated Home as the app’s entry layer. With the redesign, biometric access was added for the first time. Home also helped members return to recent features, check notifications, and reach key services.

Main path
Step 01

Access check

Password, first-time biometrics, collaborator entry and account validation.

Step 02

First visit

Short onboarding explains the new app structure before Home.

Step 03

Home hub

Recent features, notifications and frequent healthcare tasks.

Step 04

Next route

Users continue to Health, Certificates, Support or Network.

Design decisions

Reduce choices before login

The first screen focused on access. Secondary actions moved into clearer routes.

Show recent interactions

Home let members see the latest features they had used, so they could return faster.

Add useful notifications

Notifications included medical appointment reminders and confirmations for healthcare service approvals.

Healthcare service approvals

I treated this flow as a guided submission and follow-up process. Members needed to request an approval, upload the right documents, confirm their contact details, and check the status without calling support.

Request path
Step 01

Choose action

Users enter Authorisations and choose between requesting or checking.

Step 02

Select member

The request can be made for the user or someone in their family group.

Step 03

Upload documents

Users add the medical order and supporting clinical history if needed.

Step 04

Review and send

The final step lets users check documents and observations before submission.

Follow-up path
Step 01

Confirm phone

The app checks the phone number used for status updates.

Step 02

Validate input

Users are guided if the phone number, file size or file format is wrong.

Step 03

Track status

Users can consult the request instead of starting a new one.

Step 04

Receive result

The app keeps users informed when the approval number is ready.

Design decisions

Separate request and consult

The flow split new submissions from status checks to reduce repeated work.

Support family-group use

Members could request approvals for themselves or another person in their group.

Control document quality

The upload step handled camera, gallery and PDF files, with size and format checks.

Medical leave services

I treated Incapacities as a service hub with three different jobs: downloading an existing medical leave certificate, submitting a transcription request, and checking payment status. The flow had to separate those tasks while keeping each path short, clear and recoverable.

Entry path
Step 01

Open certificates

Users enter Certificates and Incapacities from the bottom navigation.

Step 02

Choose Incapacities

The section groups certificate download, transcription and payment status.

Step 03

Pick the task

Users choose whether they want a certificate, a transcription or payment status.

Step 04

Follow route

Each route asks only for the information needed to complete that task.

Certificate path
Step 01

Define recipient

If there is a family group, users choose who the certificate is for.

Step 02

Add email

The app asks where to send a copy before showing available records.

Step 03

Select record

Users choose the medical leave record they want to download.

Step 04

Download

The PDF is downloaded to the device and also sent by email.

Transcription path
Step 01

Add contact and date

Users enter email or phone and select the medical leave start date.

Step 02

Upload documents

The app accepts camera, gallery or PDF uploads with format and size checks.

Step 03

Accept terms

Users review and accept the conditions before submitting the request.

Step 04

Receive case number

A confirmation message gives the request number and expected response time.

Payment status path
Step 01

Choose query

Users check all records or filter by a date range.

Step 02

Select incapacity

The app shows eligible medical leaves so users can open one record.

Step 03

Read status

The detail explains the payment state, amount, method and key dates.

Step 04

Next action

Depending on the status, users continue, contact support or reissue payment.

Design decisions

Separate three jobs

The Incapacities hub keeps download, transcription and payment status from competing in one path.

Adapt to family groups

The certificate path changes when users need a document for someone linked to their account.

Healthcare network search

I treated the healthcare network as a guided search flow. Members needed to choose their plan, select the type of care or service, filter by location, and compare providers through list and map views before deciding where to go or who to call.

Entry path
Step 01

Open network

Users enter Healthcare Network from the bottom navigation.

Step 02

Select plan

They choose contributive, subsidised or complementary care plan.

Step 03

Continue

The main service menu appears once the plan route is selected.

Service path
Step 01

Choose category

Users choose urgent care, primary care, pharmacies or offices.

Step 02

Choose urgency type

Urgent care is split into obstetric, pediatric, psychiatric, adult and dental.

Step 03

Use same pattern

Each category keeps the same department and municipality search structure.

Step 04

Reduce relearning

The same interaction model works across clinics, pharmacies and offices.

Location path
Step 01

Select department

The user opens the department field and chooses from the available list.

Step 02

Select municipality

The second field narrows the search to the selected municipality.

Step 03

Enable search

The next button activates only after both location fields are complete.

Step 04

Show results

The app then shows matching providers for the selected service and location.

Results path
Step 01

Browse list

Users review provider cards with address, phone, opening hours and details.

Step 02

Search within results

A search field helps users find a specific provider faster.

Step 03

Open map

The map view shows the selected provider and keeps contact information close.

Step 04

Call provider

Users can continue by calling the provider directly from the result detail.

Design decisions

Standardise search

The same department and municipality pattern works across all network categories.

Separate service types

Urgencies, primary care, pharmacies, offices and Vive Digital points remain easy to scan.

Support decision making

List, map, contact details and call actions help users choose where to go next.
